<div class="era-block" id="era-1957">
  <h3>1957</h3>
  <p>Sputnik 1 was launched into space on October 4th, 1957. This is the first satellite to be launched into space. Almost a month later scientists launched another satellite, Sputnik 2, this time containing a dog named Laika. These two satellites were launched by the Soviet Union and sparked the Space Race, a race between the United States and the Soviet Union to achieve the finest space exploration capabilities.<sup>5,6</sup></p>
</div>

![Sputnik 1](/WIAA/images/sputnik.jpg)
<p class="image-caption">Sputnik 1, the first satellite launched into space.<sup>7</sup></p>

<div class="era-block" id="era-1958">
  <h3>1958</h3>
  <p>In response, the United States launched Explorer 1 about three months later, January 31st, 1958. The National Aeronautics and Space Administration was founded to coordinate American Space exploration. Explorer 3 and Sputnik 3 were also launched that year as well as Score, launched by the United States as an experimental communication satellite.<sup>5</sup></p>
</div>

<div class="era-block" id="era-1960s">
  <h3>1960s</h3>
  <p>Work over the next few years led to the launching of more satellites during the 1960s. Trios was launched in 1960 and was the first weather monitoring satellite. Telstar was launched in 1962 and was the first active communications satellite, allowing live television to be transmitted across the Atlantic Ocean. Syncom 3 was launched in 1964 to share television coverage of the Olympic Games in Tokyo Japan. Early Bird was launched in 1965 and served as the first geostationary satellite for communication purposes and allowed international phone calls and television broadcasts. Other countries entered the race this decade and challenged the United States to develop increasingly better satellites.<sup>5,8</sup></p>
</div>

<div class="era-block" id="era-1970s-1980s">
  <h3>1970s-1980s</h3>
  <p>Landsat imaging satellites were first launched in 1972 and is now the longest running continuous satellite-based Earth observation program. Global positioning satellites were initiated and the first was launched in 1978. Satellite supported television broadcasting became widespread.</p>
</div>

<div class="era-block" id="era-1990s-2000s">
  <h3>1990s-2000s</h3>
  <p>The Hubble Space Telescope was launched in 1990 to capture imagery of the universe. The International Telecommunications Satellite Organization increased global communication ability and satellite television became more widespread in consumer products. Digital Globe and GeoEye satellite systems began sharing high resolution satellite imagery. Global positioning systems (GPS) became available to consumers, and Google Earth made satellite imagery available on a public scale.</p>
</div>

<div class="era-block" id="era-2010s-present">
  <h3>2010s-Present</h3>
  <p>Small satellites and CubeSats decreased expenditure allowing companies such as Planet Labs or SpaceX to launch hundreds of satellites and create interconnected networks. SpaceX provides public access to space missions and deployed a constellation of satellites including Starlink for high-speed broadband internet.</p>
</div>
