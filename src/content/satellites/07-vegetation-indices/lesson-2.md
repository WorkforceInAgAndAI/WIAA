Sensors detect different wavelengths of light which alone can inform scientists about the health of plants and characteristics of other objects. However, when wavelengths are combined mathematically to calculate a single value index for a pixel in an image, scientists can gain a deeper understanding of relative reflectance values and correct for unwanted effects.

<div class="definitions-grid">
  <div class="definition-card">
    <h3>Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI)</h3>
    <p>One of the most used vegetation index formulas. Once calculated, a high NDVI signals healthy plants, while a low NDVI signals stressed plants. A very low NDVI value indicates no plants are present and is a sign of bare soil.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="definition-card">
    <h3>Enhanced Vegetation Index (EVI)</h3>
    <p>Includes a blue wavelength which can be used to correct aerosol scattering. High EVI values indicate healthy plants, while low EVI values indicate stressed plants.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="definition-card">
    <h3>Soil Adjusted Vegetation Index (SAVI)</h3>
    <p>Used for analysis to account for soil present in analysis. The soil brightness factor L limits the impact of soil in vegetation analysis. High SAVI indicates dense and healthy vegetation, while low SAVI indicates bare soil or limited plant material.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="definition-card">
    <h3>Green Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (GNDVI)</h3>
    <p>Includes green wavelengths in the calculation. This method is used to correct Rayleigh scattering. High GNDVI indicates healthy plants while low GNDVI indicates stressed plants.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="definition-card">
    <h3>Normalized Difference Red-Edge Index (NDRE)</h3>
    <p>Includes red edge which can be used to detect water related metrics. High NDRE indicates healthy plants while low NDRE indicates stressed plants.</p>
  </div>
</div>
