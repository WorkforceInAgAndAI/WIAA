Sensors can be used to detect wavelengths throughout the electromagnetic spectrum. Different parts of the electromagnetic spectrum provide insight into characteristics of objects beyond what human eyes can perceive.

<p>Multispectral cameras are commonly used in plant science to detect red, green, and blue wavelengths like RGB cameras, however multispectral cameras also detect the wavelengths reflected by the plant in the red edge and near-infrared (NIR) portions of the electromagnetic spectrum. These two wavelengths interact with chlorophyll and can be used as an indicator of plant health and chlorophyll density. Multispectral cameras can be used to determine the health of a plant.</p>

<p>Plants that are healthier reflect more NIR light while stressed plants reflect less NIR light. Healthy plants also reflect less red-edge light than stressed plants.<sup>34</sup></p>

<p>Visually, we see many plants as green, so in the visible portion of the electromagnetic spectrum, plants will reflect more green light than blue or red light. Blue and red light will be absorbed by the plant. Chlorophyll absorbs light in the blue and red portions of the spectrum.</p>

<p>Moving towards longer wavelengths, the red edge portion of the spectrum illustrates an incline in plant reflectance. An unhealthy plant with low chlorophyll will absorb less red light and therefore reflect more red light. Chlorophyll does not absorb red-edge wavelengths, so a large amount is reflected by the plant. If a plant is reflecting more red light, the slope of the curve from red to red edge will be less steep. The steepness of the curve between these two areas is a key indicator of the amount of chlorophyll present in plant tissues which can be used to determine relative plant health.</p>

<p>The near-infrared portion of the electromagnetic spectrum can also be used to illustrate plant health. Chlorophyll does not absorb light in the near-infrared portion of the electromagnetic spectrum like the red edge portion. Near-infrared light does interact with other portions of the vegetative tissue such as cell walls, which reflect near-infrared light when healthy. Chlorophyll, cell walls and other components of the leaf internal structure reflect or absorb light depending on the health of the plant. Combined, these structures reflect more near-infrared light when healthy, and less near-infrared light when unhealthy.</p>
