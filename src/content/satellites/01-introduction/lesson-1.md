Have you ever seen a photo of the Earth from space? Chances are high that the image you saw was taken from a satellite. But what is a satellite and what does it do? These are a few of the foundational characteristics of satellites this chapter will cover.
</div>

<p>Think of a tool that you want to be able to use all over Earth. Maybe it is a tool to take measurements, or one to help people across the world communicate. For this tool to be applied at a global scale, one method is to launch that tool into space where it can circulate Earth to transmit signals or collect data to everyone.</p>

<p>A satellite is an object that orbits another object in space. Satellites are held in a consistent orbit by the gravitational pull of the larger object it circulates and the speed at which it travels. Satellites can be naturally occurring or made by humans. The moon is an example of a naturally occurring satellite that orbits Earth while Landsat 9 is an example of a human-made object that orbits space to collect data. The term "satellites" refers to human-made satellites unless otherwise indicated in this workbook.<sup>1</sup></p>
