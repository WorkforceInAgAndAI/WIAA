Images captured by sensors on satellites are composed of <strong>pixels</strong>, and each pixel represents a spot on Earth's surface. Pixels are arranged in rows and columns and work together to visualize the specific area it represents. Phone cameras capture all pixels that comprise an image in a single shot, however, because satellites are constantly in motion, each pixel must be captured separately. The method of recording pixel values varies by sensor, but two common methods are push broom and whisk broom scanners. Hyperspectral sensors commonly rely on whisk broom and push broom scanners to capture data. RGB, multispectral, and thermal can also use whisk broom and push broom scanners, however, area array scanners, which capture the entire AOI in one shot are commonly used by RGB, multispectral, thermal, and some advanced hyperspectral sensors.

<p><strong>Whisk broom scanners</strong> systematically capture one pixel at a time, working across rows of pixels in one direction, and then working across the next row of pixels in the opposite direction. The back-and-forth motion of capturing pixels one at a time is like the back-and-forth motion of a whisk broom.</p>

<p><strong>Push broom scanners</strong> can capture a full row of pixels at a time. Many scanners fire simultaneously to capture each row of pixels before moving to the next row. The single motion sweeping motion is like the pushing motion of a push broom.</p>
