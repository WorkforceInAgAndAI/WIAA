Satellites are more than just cameras floating in space. Satellites require a control center, protection system, power source, an orientation sensor, communication system, and then the sensor. The MySat Satellite Kit contains all of these components.<sup>24</sup>

![The MySat Satellite Kit](/WIAA/images/mySat.jpg)
<p class="image-caption">MySat satellite kit.<sup>24</sup></p>

<p>The computer systems and clock work as the <strong>central control center</strong>. This acts as the brain of the satellite and controls all other parts of the satellite. The <strong>main onboard computer</strong> is the primary processor that manages software, controls sensors, and manages data. The <strong>auxiliary onboard computer</strong> is responsible for secondary satellite systems, and the clock is used to accurately record data timestamps.<sup>24</sup></p>

<p>The control center and all other components are contained in the <strong>frame</strong> of the satellite. This helps to protect the satellite components from damage and provides a secure location to connect all components together. Satellites may also contain additional <strong>insulation or reflective features</strong> to prevent damage from extreme temperature fluctuations.<sup>24</sup></p>

<p>Satellites in space need to be self-sufficient when it comes to power. <strong>Solar panels</strong> are a useful method used to convert solar energy into electrical energy. Once the electrical energy is converted, the <strong>battery</strong> stores the energy for when the solar energy is blocked from the satellite. The <strong>battery controller and voltage and current monitor</strong> regulate charging and tracks power usage.<sup>24</sup></p>

<p>When data is collected, it is important to track the origin of the data. Data points such as time of data collection, coordinates of the satellite in space, orientation of the satellite and sensor, and movement of the satellite can be used to process and provide context to the collected data. The <strong>gyroscope</strong> and <strong>accelerometer</strong> are used to determine the orientation and movement of the sensor. <strong>Photosensors</strong> also help to determine the orientation of the satellite but do so using light positioning. The <strong>servo motor</strong> is a tool that can respond to the orientation of the satellite and adjust the satellite to the intended orientation.<sup>24</sup></p>

<p>The central control center acts as the brain to coordinate satellite activities, however the <strong>communication system</strong> is vital for receiving and sending signals from ground stations on Earth which can impact the delegations sent out by the central control center. The <strong>radio transceiver</strong> sends and receives radio signals, and the <strong>antenna</strong> supports the transceiver to broadcast signals from the ground station.<sup>24</sup></p>

<p>Finally, with all the features in place to control the satellite, the <strong>payload</strong>, or the primary data collection devices must also be included on the satellite. Payloads vary by satellite; many have cameras that capture images of Earth. Some light sensors specialize in certain wavelengths. Other environmental sensors may also be included on the satellite to measure factors such as temperature, humidity, or air pressure.<sup>24</sup></p>
