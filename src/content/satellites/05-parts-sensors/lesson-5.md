Satellite missions require large monetary investments, so efficient use of the space available on the satellite platforms is crucial to get the most out of investments. While adding as many sensors as possible to a platform would help researchers to gather a lot of data, there are important considerations that guide sensor placement.

<p>The satellite mission itself and the objectives of the mission are the primary guide for sensors to be included on the platform. A mission with objectives to explore plant health with multispectral imagery may not gain valuable input from a LiDAR sensor.</p>

<p>Sensor size is also crucial to consider. Small satellites require smaller sensors than what may be able to fit on a larger satellite. The amount of space available for data storage can also consider the type of sensor that can be included on the platform. Hyperspectral produces large amounts of data compared to RGB sensors. Some satellites such as LiDAR require high amount of energy to power and are not reasonable to use for continuous data collection.</p>

<p>The projected environment of the satellite in space will determine the protective techniques are needed and if they can be added to the satellite. Temperature conditions expected at the satellite's orbit altitude are one example. Potential atmospheric interactions may post different challenges depending on the orbit altitude of the satellite.</p>
