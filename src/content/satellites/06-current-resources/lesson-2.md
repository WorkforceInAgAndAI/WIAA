<div class="ethics-block" id="ethics-privacy">
  <p>One concern about satellite data is protections or privacy issues about imagery over certain locations. For example, some countries have expressed wanting control or restrictions over satellite data that is collected over their region. Sensitive areas such as military sites, borders, or agricultural areas are the primary locations of concern. Another argument is wealthier countries may have access to data inaccessible to poorer countries due to relative cost of data, internet access, or infrastructure availability for accessing and processing. Unequal access to data can be narrowed to a local scale, where a large corporation may view privately owned land using imagery inaccessible to local landowners due to cost or availability.</p>
</div>

<div class="ethics-block" id="ethics-conflicting">
  <p>Space data ethics explored this further by examining how to handle data that can impact people globally. The same data from satellite imagery can be used by people of opposite parties for conflicting reasons.</p>
</div>

<div class="ethics-block" id="ethics-protections">
  <p>Protections over the data accessed are also essential to consider. Free access to data can be used and shared without restrictions, however data from private companies often has restrictions over what the data can be used for, how the data can be used, and where or how the data and data findings can be shared.</p>
</div>

<div class="ethics-block" id="ethics-sovereignty">
  <p>Data sovereignty is the idea that a country, group, or person has rights over data concerning their own land. Outer space is a global common, no countries have ownership of outer space or the data collected from there. This means that data can be collected over any area of the world without restrictions. The combination of data sovereignty and outer space as a global common prompt the discussion of ethics surrounding these issues.</p>
</div>

<p>Satellite data is different from many other data sources and can provide powerful information. However, it poses new complex questions around ethical use.</p>
