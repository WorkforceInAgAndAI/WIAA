Temporal resolution refers to the frequency that a satellite passes over the same location. Landsat has a temporal resolution of 16 days, meaning a Landsat satellite will pass over the same location to capture imagery once every 16 days which can be helpful for tracking seasonal crop growth. Planet Labs's SkySats has an average constellation temporal resolution of 6 to 7 times per day, meaning of the 21 SkySats in orbit, 6-7 of the satellites will pass over the same spot. If a user wants to mitigate effects of different satellites and sensor calibrations, the individual satellites have an average temporal resolution of 4 to 5 days. This higher temporal resolution of the SkySats allows farmers to track daily changes in crop development. The temporal resolution of a satellite depends on the speed of the satellite, altitude above Earth, and number of satellites in orbit.

<p>Temporal resolution is also impacted by orbit pattern or the route that the satellite travels around the Earth.</p>

<div class="definitions-grid">
  <div class="definition-card">
    <h3>Polar Orbiting</h3>
    <p>Pass over the North and South Poles and pass over every part of the Earth. These satellites are in low earth orbit.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="definition-card">
    <h3>Geostationary</h3>
    <p>Orbit the Earth at the same rotation speed as the Earth so the satellite remains fixed over the same spot, providing continuous coverage of one area. These satellites orbit at a high earth orbit of 35,769 kilometers above Earth.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="definition-card">
    <h3>Sun-Synchronous</h3>
    <p>Pass over the same spot on Earth at the same time each day. These satellites operate at low Earth orbit.</p>
  </div>
</div>
