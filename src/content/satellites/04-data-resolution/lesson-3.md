Energy moves in waves and the length and frequency of the waves determines how the energy can be used. The electromagnetic spectrum is a way to visualize the types of energy.

<p>We can look at portions of the electromagnetic spectrum as either specific measurements or ranges. Wavelengths are specific measurements, such as 450 nm while bands are regions of the electromagnetic spectrum where the strength of the signal from the electromagnetic spectrum peaks at a specific wavelength.</p>

![Bands and wavelengths](/WIAA/images/bands_wavelengths.jpg)
<p class="image-caption">Representation of bands and wavelengths in the electromagnetic spectrum.</p>

<p>Some longer wavelengths can be used for communication, like radio waves, some wavelengths can be used for heating or communication such as microwaves. Infrared waves can be used for heating or thermal detection. Shorter wavelengths such as ultraviolet light can be used for degradation of materials or sanitation. Similarly, Gamma Rays can be used for sterilization or astronomical research. X-ray wavelengths are often used for imaging or quality inspection of materials. These wavelengths are not easily detectable by humans unless assisted by specialized tools. A small section of the electromagnetic spectrum is detectible by humans and is referred to as visible light. This consists of red, green, and blue wavelengths and their combinations.</p>

![The electromagnetic spectrum](/WIAA/images/electromagnetic_spectrum.jpg)
<p class="image-caption">The electromagnetic spectrum and a representation of the wavelengths across the spectrum.</p>

<p>Shorter wavelengths have more energy than longer wavelengths. Every object can reflect, absorb, emit, or transmit light in different quantities, and the interaction of light with any object can tell us more about that object. When these wavelengths interact with an object, the energy carried by the wave is distributed by reflection, absorption, emittance, or transmittance. Energy that is absorbed by the object is converted into a new form. Snow is an example of an object that reflects visible light, which causes it to appear white because all wavelengths are reflected equally. When light interacts with snow, the incident radiation or the incoming radiation from the sun is modified only slightly as most of the energy is reflected, while only a small portion is absorbed and converted into heat. Dark objects such as asphalt appear black because the materials that compose asphalt absorb lots of light across the visible portion of the electromagnetic spectrum. Asphalt absorbs a lot of incident radiation, and the absorbed energy is re-emitted as thermal energy which has a longer wavelength than the incident radiation from the sun in the visible portion of the electromagnetic spectrum. Any object with a temperature emits light, although the visibility of the light to humans varies by the temperature of the object. Lightbulbs and rocks both emit light, however the light emitted by lightbulbs is visible to humans. Some objects such as glass allow light to pass through them or be transmitted. Sometimes when light is transmitted through an object, the material of the object bends the light as it passes through, such as in a prism, this is referred to as refraction. Light can interact with matter in a combination of these ways to create the diverse appearances of objects we experience each day. A blue coffee mug reflects mostly blue light while absorbing red and green light. A red tinted flower vase will let most light pass through but will reflect some red light while absorbing blue and green light. Asphalt will absorb lots of visible light and will emit light in the form of heat.</p>

![Light interactions with objects](/WIAA/images/light_interactions.jpg)
<p class="image-caption">Interactions of light with common objects. Red arrows indicate red light, blue arrows indicate blue light, green arrows indicate green light, white arrows indicate heat emitted, yellow arrows indicate heat generated by the object, and dark red arrows indicate near infrared radiation.</p>

<p>When looking at a plant, sometime the appearance of leaves can inform us about the plant's health. We can use specialized tools and math to help us to calculate indexes that can fine tune these plant health observations. Red-edge and near-infrared wavelengths are two wavelengths that provide additional information about plant health based on the amount of light that is reflected from the plant.</p>

<p>Sensors can be attached to satellites or drones to detect the light reflected by objects from the above perspective. Often these sensors can be used to detect a variety of light reflectance. Some sensors detect just three bands, some detect 6 bands, and others detect hundreds to thousands of bands. The more bands a sensor detects, the higher the spectral resolution.</p>
