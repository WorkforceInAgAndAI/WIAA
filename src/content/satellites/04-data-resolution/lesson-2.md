Spatial resolution is a measure of the area represented by each pixel in an image. Satellites equipped with cameras capture raster images. Most images are rasters, which refer to images that are composed of pixels. Each pixel in a satellite image represents a small area on the Earth. The amount of area that the pixel represents is referred to as the ground sampling distance (GSD). A satellite with a GSD of 30 meters means the reflectance values shown in the pixel represent the average reflectance values for a 30 by 30-meter area on the Earth's surface. Smaller GSD values indicate higher spatial resolution because a smaller ground area is represented by each pixel.

<p>Drones often achieve higher spatial resolution than satellites, allowing for intensive field monitoring, however drones are limited by the area they can cover and require weather conditions conducive to flying. While satellites can cover vast areas of land, allowing analysis and comparisons across large scale areas in near unison, they face challenges with cloud cover during poor weather conditions. Certain wavelengths can penetrate cloud cover to help resolve this challenge.</p>
