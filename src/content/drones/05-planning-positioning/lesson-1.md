When using a drone to record data, pilots can use mission planning apps to help maintain consistency during the mission.


## What Shapes a Flight Plan
The pilot should consider the qualities of the subject and the trait of interest and the level of detail required to extract meaningful data. 

<div class="benefits-grid">
  <div class="benefit-card">
    <div class="benefit-number">1</div>
    <h4>Altitude & Speed</h4>
    <p>In plant science, a drone pilot may need to fly at a lower altitude and speed to capture highly detailed data. Some fields may be very large, so a pilot may choose to fly at a higher altitude and at a quicker speed to cover the area quicker. </p>
  </div>
  <div class="benefit-card">
    <div class="benefit-number">2</div>
    <h4>Terrain</h4>
    <p>The landscape of the field should also be considered. If the land is highly sloped, the flight plan should be set to adjust for major changes in altitude to ensure consistent height across the field. </p>
  </div>
  <div class="benefit-card">
    <div class="benefit-number">3</div>
    <h4>Lighting</h4>
    <p>Missions flown for the purpose of collecting spectral imagery should be conducted around solar noon (between 10 am and 2 pm) with minimal or even cloud cover to ensure consistent lighting. </p>
  </div>
</div>

## Sensor, Battery, and Software Trade-offs

The sensor attached to the UAV is an important factor to prioritize to ensure the sensor can maintain quality during the flight of the UAV. The capabilities of the UAV and flight planning software are a major determining factor for the flight.


<div class="benefits-grid">
  <div class="benefit-card">
    <div class="benefit-number">1</div>
    <h4>Battery and Altitude</h4>
    <p>A UAV with a long-lasting battery can handle flying at a lower altitude for a longer timer, where a UAV with a shorter lasting batter would handle flying at a higher altitude for a shorter period better. Flight planning software often emphasizes adjusting the speed of the UAV, and the altitude of the flight.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="benefit-card">
    <div class="benefit-number">2</div>
    <h4>Overlap</h4>
    <p>Front and side overlap of the imagery can be adjusted but is a trade-off between time and battery life available for the flight and the quality of the data needed for the target data.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="benefit-card">
    <div class="benefit-number">3</div>
    <h4>Software</h4>
    <p>The pilot should consider the capabilities of the software that will be used to stitch, and extract the data captured by the sensor after the mission. </p>
  </div>
</div>
