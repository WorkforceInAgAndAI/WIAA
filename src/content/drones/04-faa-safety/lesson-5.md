Drones can be flown at a maximum altitude of 400 feet above ground level (AGL). This limitation is set in place to avoid interfering with manned aircraft and mitigate risk of drone accidents.

## Uncontrolled Airspace


This section of the national airspace is often part of **Class G airspace**, or uncontrolled airspace, where air traffic control (ATC) does not monitor or control the airspace. Class G airspace generally spans from the ground surface to 1,200 ft AGL where class E airspace begins.




## Controlled Airspace

Above Class G airspace or across the country where ATC support is available is controlled airspace. Class A, B, C, D, and E airspace designate different areas of the airspace where aircraft travel. Airspace classes can vary based on context, but general airspace classifications are provided. Class B, C, and D typically begin at the ground surface and extend up to different heights depending on the air traffic surrounding airports and all have operational control towers.

<div class="impact-callout">

&#x20; <h4>Special Use Airspace</h4>

&#x20; <p>**Special use airspace** exists over a variety of high importance or risk areas which often have limits on flight activity. Prohibited areas block aircraft entirely for security reasons. **Restricted areas** and **warning areas**  impose flight regulations due to flight hazards however vary in that restricted areas are regulated by U.S. federal laws while warning areas occur off the U.S. coast and may be in domestic and/ or international waters. **Military Operation Areas** indicate areas and usage timings of military training, preventing interference with non-military air traffic. **Alert areas** indicate airspace with high traffic or other unusual airspace activity.</p>
