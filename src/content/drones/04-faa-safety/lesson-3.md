Just like owning and driving a vehicle, drones must be registered and identifiable.



<div class="faa-info">

&#x20; <p>Once every three years any U.S. citizen or permanent resident above the age of 13 can register a drone for $5. The drone registration must be in the operator’s possession each time the drone is flown, and the drone must be labeled with the registration number. </p>

</div>



## Remote ID



Drones must also be equipped with a **Remote ID** which is a way for the drone to be identified by others by the detection of a signal broadcasted by the drone. This can work two ways:



<div class="benefits-grid">

&#x20; <div class="benefit-card">

&#x20;   <div class="benefit-number">1</div>

&#x20;   <h4>Built-in Standard Remote ID</h4>

&#x20;   <p>Broadcasts the drone's information for the entire duration of the mission.</p>

&#x20; </div>

&#x20; <div class="benefit-card">

&#x20;   <div class="benefit-number">2</div>

&#x20;   <h4>Broadcast Module</h4>

&#x20;   <p>An attached module thatbroadcasts information about the drone the entire duration of the mission but must occur within line-of sight of the pilot. </p>

&#x20; </div>

</div>

![Remote ID](/WIAA/images/remote_ID.jpg)
<p class="image-caption">Remote identification options for drones.<sup>6</sup></p>


<div class="impact-callout">

&#x20; <h4>No Remote ID?</h4>

&#x20; <p>If a Remote ID is not built in or attached to the drone, the drone is limited to operations within a FAA-Recognized Identification Area FIRA. This area allows drones without identification broadcasted to still be operated, if the pilot and the drone are both within the FRIA zone and the drone is within line of sight of the pilot. </p>

</div>
