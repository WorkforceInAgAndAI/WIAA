The <strong>electromagnetic spectrum</strong> is the entire range of electromagnetic radiation. All objects with a temperature emit radiation at some frequency. Radiation is emitted at different wavelengths, where each <strong>wavelength</strong> is a specific point on the electromagnetic spectrum. Different wavelengths can be used to transmit messages, indicate stress, sterilize objects, and many other tasks. In plant science, researchers can use different ranges of wavelengths in a portion of the electromagnetic spectrum, called <strong>bands</strong>, to determine plant health, or detect early symptoms of stress.

<div class="impact-callout">

## Spectral Resolution

  <p class="section-intro"><strong>Spectral resolution</strong> is a measurement of a sensor's ability to distinguish between different wavelengths of light. A sensor with a high spectral resolution, such as a hyperspectral camera can distinguish between many types of light, while a sensor with a low spectral resolution, such as an RGB camera, can only distinguish between a few wavelengths.</p>
</div>

Drones can be equipped with various <strong>payloads</strong> or attachments that serve a purpose, often for data collection. Spectral sensors are a type of payload commonly attached to drones. Some of the most used sensors in agriculture are RGB cameras, multispectral cameras, hyperspectral cameras, thermal cameras, and LiDAR sensors. Each sensor can be used to collect different types of data at varying levels of spectral resolution.

## Some cameras are passive and some are active.

<div class="benefits-grid">
  <div class="benefit-card">
    <div class="benefit-number">P</div>
    <h4>Passive Sensors</h4>
    <p>Passive sensors detect wavelengths detected from a source other than itself. RGB, multispectral, hyperspectral, and thermal cameras are examples of passive sensors.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="benefit-card">
    <div class="benefit-number">A</div>
    <h4>Active Sensors</h4>
    <p>Active sensors emit their own wavelengths and then detect the same wavelength they produced. LiDAR sensors are active sensors.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="sensor-cards">
  <div class="sensor-card">
    <h4>RGB Camera</h4>
    <p>Like your phone camera, produce images that use red, green, and blue light reflectance. These sensors are commonly used on drones because they are inexpensive and can achieve high resolution. RGB cameras can be used to determine plant height, count plants or plant parts, differentiate crops from weeds, among other traits.<sup>1</sup></p>
  </div>

  <div class="sensor-card">
    <h4>Multispectral Camera</h4>
    <p>Typically consist of 6 to 10 sensors that detect light reflected from specific broad areas of the electromagnetic spectrum. Some multispectral cameras allow specific bands of interest to be chosen, but often multispectral cameras detect red, green, blue, red-edge, and near infrared wavelengths. Red-edge and near infrared wavelengths are used especially in plant science to determine plant health because chlorophyll has a peak reflectance in the near infrared portion of the electromagnetic spectrum and has a steep reflectance increase in the red-edge portion.<sup>1</sup></p>
  </div>

  <div class="sensor-card">
    <h4>Hyperspectral Camera</h4>
    <p>Sensors that detect light reflected from hundreds of narrow areas of the electromagnetic spectrum. The data from hyperspectral sensors can provide highly detailed information about the objects captured by the sensor. Because of the high spectral resolution, detailed information on plant health to the biochemical level can be extracted to indicate early symptoms of plant stress. Hyperspectral cameras do suffer the tradeoff between spectral and spatial resolution, as they have limited spatial resolution. These cameras also demand high power and require more detailed downstream data analysis.<sup>1</sup></p>
  </div>

  <div class="sensor-card">
    <h4>Thermal Camera</h4>
    <p>Measures the amount of light emitted from an object in the form of infrared radiation. Thermal data is useful to researchers because it can be used as an indicator of plant health. When a plant is stressed, photosynthesis and transpiration are often reduced, therefore resulting in warmer temperatures of the crop canopy. Practical usage of thermal cameras is limited by cost, sensor integration on drones, and need to correct for thermal emission by surrounding objects.<sup>1</sup></p>
  </div>

  <div class="sensor-card">
    <h4>LiDAR Sensor</h4>
    <p>Can be used to measure the distance between the object and the sensor. LiDAR sensors emit a laser that travels to an object, reflects off the object, and travels back to the LiDAR sensor. The resulting data from the sensor forms a point cloud, or many datapoint organized into a 3-dimentional space by X, Y, and Z coordinates. Plant height or biomass can be determined from LiDAR data, although require high amounts of data processing.<sup>1</sup></p>
  </div>
</div>
