When setting the camera parameters, researchers must consider the level of detail needed for the level of resolution needed. The <strong>ground sampling distance (GSD)</strong> is the amount of area represented by one pixel. For example, if one pixel has a GSD of 2 centimeters, this means one side of the pixel covers 4 square centimeters (2 cm * 2 cm = 4 square cm). Four parameters impact the GSD of an image.

![Ground Sampling Distance](/WIAA/images/gsd.jpg)
<p class="image-caption">Ground sampling distance calculation visual.<sup>17</sup></p>


<div class="benefits-grid">
  <div class="benefit-card">
    <div class="benefit-number">1</div>
    <h4>Height</h4>
    <p>The first is height. As the altitude of the sensor increases, a larger area is captured by each pixel. Because a larger area of the ground is covered, the spatial resolution of the image decreases because less detail can be extracted from the image.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="benefit-card">
    <div class="benefit-number">2</div>
    <h4>Sensor Width</h4>
    <p>The sensor width is the size of the sensor inside the camera. A larger sensor increases the GSD by increasing the area on the sensor covered by each pixel, thereby reducing the spatial resolution.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="benefit-card">
    <div class="benefit-number">3</div>
    <h4>Focal Length</h4>
    <p>The focal length also impacts the GSD. The focal length is the distance between the sensor and the focal point. A longer focal length decreases the field of view, which decreases the area needed to be covered by each pixel. This results in a higher spatial resolution, and images with finer details. If the focal length is doubled, then the area covered by each pixel is halved.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="benefit-card">
    <div class="benefit-number">4</div>
    <h4>Image Width</h4>
    <p>The last factor is image width which refers to the number of pixels wide the output image is. When increased, the image width results in a decreased GSD. This works because a higher number of pixels will display more detail than fewer pixels covering the same area.</p>
  </div>
</div>
