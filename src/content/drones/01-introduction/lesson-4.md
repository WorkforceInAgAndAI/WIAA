During operation, UAS must balance the opposing forces of lift, weight, drag, and thrust to create a stable flight.

![Stress Revealed](/WIAA/images/forces_of_flight.jpg)
<p class="image-caption">The four forces of flight on an unmanned aerial system.</p>




## The Four Forces of Flight



<div class="benefits-grid">

&#x20; <div class="benefit-card">

&#x20;   <div class="benefit-number">1</div>

&#x20;   <h4>Lift</h4>

&#x20;   <p>The controlled upward force created by the UAS's propellers, typically controlled remotely by the operator.</p>

&#x20; </div>



&#x20; <div class="benefit-card">

&#x20;   <div class="benefit-number">2</div>

&#x20;   <h4>Weight</h4>

&#x20;   <p>The downward pull of gravity on the UAS. This is the force that lift has to overcome for the drone to fly.</p>

&#x20; </div>



&#x20; <div class="benefit-card">

&#x20;   <div class="benefit-number">3</div>

&#x20;   <h4>Thrust</h4>

&#x20;   <p>The force that propels the UAS in a forward direction. This is also controlled remotely by the operator.</p>

&#x20; </div>



&#x20; <div class="benefit-card">

&#x20;   <div class="benefit-number">4</div>

&#x20;   <h4>Drag</h4>

&#x20;   <p>The atmosphere's resistance to the UAS's movement. The atmosphere is fluid, like water, so objects, such as UAS, must push around air molecules to move.</p>

&#x20; </div>

</div>


<div class="impact-callout">

&#x20; <h4>Beyond the Four Forces</h4>

&#x20; <p>Lift, weight, drag, and thrust are key forces that influence the upward, downward, and side-to-the-side movement of the UAS; however, additional factors also influence the flight of the UAS. </p>

</div>
